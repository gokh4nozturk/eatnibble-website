Privacy Policy

Last updated: 19 October 2026

eatnibble is a calorie and macro tracker. This page explains, in plain terms, what happens to your data when you use the app.

What we process to analyze a meal

When you take or pick a photo, or type a description of what you ate, that content is sent to our backend, which forwards it to Google's Gemini API for analysis. The photo or text is processed in memory to produce a nutrition estimate (calories, protein, carbs, fat) and is not written to a database or kept after the request completes. It is not linked to your account.

Signing in

You can analyze your first few meals on a device without signing in. After that, eatnibble asks you to sign in with Apple so your daily analysis limit follows your account instead of the device.

Sign in with Apple is the only method — there is no password, and you choose whether to share your real email or use Apple's private relay address. On our backend we store a user record containing the identifier Apple gives us, that email or relay address, and a per-day count of how many analyses you've run (used only to enforce the daily limit). Your meals, photos and descriptions are never attached to this record — the only exception is the optional AI assistant connection described below, and only while you use it.

What stays on your device

Your meal log, the photos attached to your meals, your daily water count, your reminder settings and your profile (height, weight, age, sex, activity level, goal, water goal) are stored only in the app's local storage on your device, along with your sign-in session token. They are never uploaded to us unless you connect an AI assistant (see the next section), and even then only the limited summary described there. If you delete the app or clear your log in Settings, that local data is gone — we don't keep a copy anywhere.

Export data (Profile → Settings) creates a file of your profile, log and water history on your device. Where it goes from there is up to you — it's only shared through the iOS share sheet when you choose to.

Connecting an AI assistant (optional)

You can connect an AI assistant that supports the Model Context Protocol — for example ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok or Cursor — so it can log meals for you and answer questions about your day. Connecting is entirely optional, requires signing in, and happens only when you approve the assistant's 6-digit code in the app (Profile → Settings → AI Connections). Nothing below applies if you never connect one.

While at least one assistant is connected, our backend stores:

  • The connection: the assistant's name, when you connected it, and the security tokens it uses — stored only as one-way hashes, never in readable form. The same applies to a personal key, if you create one for developer tools.
  • Meals the assistant logs: the meal name, time, food items with their estimated grams and calories/macros, and any note. They wait on our backend only until the eatnibble app next opens and adds them to your log, and are then deleted from our backend.
  • A summary of your log: so the assistant can answer questions like "what did I eat today?", the app uploads your time zone, today's date and the meals of the last 7 days (name, time and calorie/macro totals — no photos). Your profile and daily goal are not included. Each update replaces the previous one. The app doesn't upload it at all while no assistant is connected, and we delete it when you remove your last connection.

Never shared with an assistant or stored on our backend: your meal photos, and any data from Apple Health (such as calories burned, water, height, weight or age).

The assistant itself is a third-party service. What you tell it, and what it reads through eatnibble when you ask it to, is processed by its provider (for example OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI or Anysphere) under that provider's own terms and privacy policy — we don't control how they handle it.

You can disconnect an assistant at any time in AI Connections; it immediately loses access, and removing the last one deletes the summary from our backend.

Photos

Photos you take with the in-app camera are also saved to an "eatnibble" album in your Photos library, so you keep a copy. eatnibble asks only for add-only access for this — it can't see or read the rest of your library. Photos you pick from your library are read only once, for the meal you're logging.

Reminders

Meal and water reminders are scheduled as local notifications on your device. No push server is involved, and no reminder data is sent to us.

Apple Health

Apple Health access is optional. If you grant permission, eatnibble reads:

  • your height, weight, date of birth (to work out your age) and sex, to fill in your profile and calculate your daily goal;
  • the active energy you've burned today and your walking and running distance, to add what you've burned to your remaining calorie budget (distance is only used for an estimate when no active energy has synced yet);
  • the water you've logged today, including water logged by other apps, to keep your water count in step.

eatnibble writes only one thing to Apple Health: water. Each glass you tap in the app is saved as a water entry, and removing a glass removes the entry eatnibble created — it never deletes water logged by other apps. The app also asks for permission to write height and weight, but doesn't currently write them.

Health data stays on your device and is never sent to our backend, to a connected AI assistant or any other third party, nor used for advertising. You can decline any of these permissions without losing the rest of the app, and change them at any time in the Health app or iOS Settings.

Third-party services

We use Google's Gemini API to analyze the photo or text you submit; Google's own terms and privacy practices apply to that processing. Sign in with Apple is handled by Apple. If you connect an AI assistant, its provider processes your conversations with it under its own terms, as described above. Our backend is hosted on Vercel. We don't use any analytics, advertising or tracking SDKs in the app.

Deleting your data

Clear log (Profile → Settings) erases the meal entries, meal photos and water history stored on your device (photos already saved to your Photos album stay there until you delete them). Delete Account (Profile → Settings) permanently deletes your backend account, its daily analysis history and everything stored for AI assistant connections (connections, personal key, pending meals and the summary) — this can't be undone. Deleting the app removes all remaining local data. Revoking eatnibble's access to your Apple ID in iOS Settings signs you out the next time you open the app.

Children's privacy

eatnibble is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them.
